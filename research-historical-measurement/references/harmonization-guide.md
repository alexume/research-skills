## 1. Money and prices

Record currency unit, metallic content or exchange regime when relevant, valuation date, market or administered price, fiscal versus calendar year, and geographic price basis. Choose a deflator that matches the economic concept. Report nominal and real series when interpretation benefits. Do not splice currencies or price indices solely by rebasing their levels.

## 2. Physical quantities and material flows

Distinguish mass, volume, energy, area, monetary value, and embodied content. Document moisture, grade, species, density, calorific value, and conversion-factor domain. Define system boundaries for extraction, imports, exports, processing, losses, recycling, and stock change. Avoid double counting intermediate products.

## 3. Population and rates

Define numerator, denominator, population at risk, exposure time, census date, and interpolation. Per-capita measures can change because the numerator, denominator, or both change. Standardize age or composition when the claim requires it; do not use total population as a universal exposure denominator.

## 4. Classifications

Create crosswalks that retain original categories and identify exact, partial, split, merged, and unmatched mappings. Do not force equivalence between legal status, occupation, land use, commodity, disease, race, or ethnicity categories whose meanings changed. Use sensitivity groupings where several mappings are defensible.

## 5. Territorial boundaries

Define target geography and reference date. Record boundary changes, parent-child relationships, annexations, splits, and unmatched areas. Choose weights based on the measured object: population weights do not automatically suit land, output, or ecological flows. Report crosswalk coverage and conservation of totals.

## 6. Time and frequency

Distinguish event date, observation date, publication date, fiscal year, agricultural year, hydrological year, and retrospective reference period. Aggregating irregular observations to annual frequency can imply false coverage. State whether interpolation is descriptive, analytical, or prohibited.

## 7. Missingness, censoring, and reconstruction

Keep distinct codes for zero, not applicable, unrecorded, illegible, suppressed, censored, lost, and structurally absent. Document imputation models and variables used. Avoid using outcomes or future treatment to impute causal baseline measures. Preserve reconstructed series separately and report sensitivity to excluding them.
