# Historical Source-Type Guide

## 1. Population and agricultural censuses

Check enumeration purpose, de jure versus de facto population, household definition, excluded populations, undercount, age heaping, occupational categories, racial or legal classifications, geographic boundaries, enumeration date, revisions, and comparability across census rounds. Published tables may differ from schedules and later harmonized datasets.

## 2. Fiscal, customs, and trade records

Identify taxable versus actual activity, tariff classification, valuation basis, declared versus inspected quantities, smuggling incentives, port coverage, transit and re-export treatment, fiscal-year boundaries, currency, official prices, and changes in administrative capacity. Customs records measure administratively visible flows, not automatically total trade or material extraction.

## 3. Land, cadastral, and property records

Distinguish ownership, possession, use, taxation, title claims, concessions, and surveyed area. Check communal, public, informal, contested, and untitled land; parcel splitting; valuation purpose; cadastral updating; boundary change; and whose rights the record recognized.

## 4. Corporate and estate records

Assess accounting purpose, surviving firms, fiscal or managerial incentives, consolidation rules, related parties, inventories, depreciation, labor categories, physical versus monetary quantities, and whether a firm or estate can represent a sector or region.

## 5. Government reports and administrative statistics

Reconstruct the reporting chain from local office to published aggregate. Check political targets, jurisdiction, reporting compliance, copied values, estimates presented as counts, institutional reorganization, administrative capacity, and retrospective revisions.

## 6. Newspapers and printed public discourse

Separate event evidence from prevalence. Assess ownership, audience, censorship, political alignment, genre, reprinting, geographic reach, named versus anonymous authorship, and whether repeated reports derive from one original dispatch.

## 7. Maps and spatial records

Record producer, purpose, survey method, projection, scale, date, positional accuracy, symbol definitions, omissions, and later georeferencing. A mapped boundary or land cover can be normative, proposed, generalized, or observed.

## 8. Environmental and scientific observations

Check instrument, sampling site, protocol, seasonality, calibration, taxonomic definition, observer changes, missing intervals, spatial representativeness, and retrospective reconstruction. Scientific standardization can improve comparability while obscuring local practices and historical category changes.
