# Example Hypothesis Memo

```md
# Frontier Allocation As A Substitute For Reform

## Question
Did frontier land allocation substitute for stronger land reform, and did it later reinforce unequal land access and conflict?

## Working Hypothesis
Frontier allocation absorbed agrarian pressure without solving structural inequality.

## Scope Of This Memo
This memo evaluates the hypothesis using structured literature notes and source registries already present in the KnowledgeWiki. It does not rely on raw PDFs or extracted-text dumps.

## Corpus Used

- literature notes:
  - `KnowledgeWiki/literature_notes/example_frontier_land_note.md`
  - `KnowledgeWiki/literature_notes/example_reform_agency_note.md`
- historical-source notes:
  - `KnowledgeWiki/historical_sources/identified_sources.md`
- prior memos:
  - `KnowledgeWiki/hypothesis_tests/example_frontier_memo.md` secondary synthesis only

## Core Findings
The corpus supports the claim that frontier allocation functioned as an alternative to deeper redistribution more strongly than it supports a claim of consistent state intentionality across all periods. The strongest support concerns unequal titling and later dispossession. The environmental arm remains plausible but under-documented in the current note set.

## Evidence Supporting The Hypothesis
Notes focused on adjudication and colonization show that frontier expansion could coexist with increasing concentration. Notes on reform agencies show that colonization was often preferred over direct redistribution. The source registry points to archives that could verify whether this was explicit policy or an aggregated effect of repeated decisions.

## Evidence Weakening The Hypothesis
The current corpus may document functional substitution more clearly than conscious design. It may also show absorption of rural population more clearly than actual containment of political violence. If those distinctions are ignored, the hypothesis becomes too strong for the available evidence.

## Mechanism Assessment
The best-supported mechanism is:
colonization -> land improvement and valuation -> elite titling or legal capture -> colonist dispossession -> durable agrarian conflict.

The weaker mechanism is:
state intentionally uses frontier land as a deliberate pressure-release valve in every period.

## Competing Explanations
Some unequal outcomes may derive from older agrarian structures rather than frontier allocation alone. Some later violence may be better explained by partisan conflict unless the memo shows how agrarian dispossession mediated escalation.

## Weak Links And Unresolved Gaps
The memo cannot yet show direct ecological measures or definitive proof of state intentionality in all periods. It should therefore separate supported institutional effects from inferred political design.

## Historical Sources To Pursue
Legislative debates, land adjudication correspondence, and reform-agency archives are the most promising next sources for checking intent, implementation, and beneficiary patterns.

## Provisional Verdict
The hypothesis is promising if reformulated around functional substitution, unequal titling, and dispossession. It is weaker when framed as a uniformly intentional policy or when it claims direct environmental outcomes without additional fichas.

## Cautions
Do not use prior memos as primary evidence. Do not treat the environmental arm as confirmed without environmental-history notes.

## Next Step
Expand the corpus with targeted environmental and legislative notes, then rerun the memo with a tighter periodization.
```
