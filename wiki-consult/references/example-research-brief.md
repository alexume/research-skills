# Research Brief

## Working Question
Did public-land distribution in a frontier region operate as a substitute for redistributive reform, and did it later reinforce inequality and conflict?

## Provisional Hypothesis
Public-land allocation absorbed agrarian pressure in the short run but reproduced unequal land access and later conflict.

## Refined Hypothesis
Public-land distribution worked as a functional substitute for stronger redistributive reform, but its medium-run effects depended on who secured title, how colonists were displaced, and whether frontier incorporation favored large holders over small occupiers.

## Subclaims

1. Frontier allocation relieved pressure for direct redistribution.
2. Colonization did not democratize land access.
3. Dispossession after colonization mediated later conflict.
4. Environmental deterioration is plausible but may not be directly documented in the current corpus.

## Mechanism Map

| Subclaim or pattern | Mechanism | Actors | Institutions | Timing | Scale | Support status |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| unequal land access persists | colonists improve land, elites secure title, colonists are displaced | colonists, landholders, local officials | land laws, courts, registries | medium run | regional | strong |
| reform is displaced by frontier expansion | state channels pressure outward instead of redistributing estates | state agencies, landholders, peasant organizations | colonization agencies, reform laws | short to medium run | national or regional | medium |

## Weak Links

- label: intentionality not demonstrated
  problem: the corpus shows functional substitution more clearly than explicit state design
  implication: avoid claiming a fully conscious strategy unless a source registry points to direct policy debate
  action: inspect legislative debates and administrative archives

- label: environmental claim thin
  problem: the corpus implies frontier conversion but lacks direct ecological metrics
  implication: keep environmental conclusions provisional
  action: add environmental-history fichas before strengthening the claim

## Evidence Gaps

- gap: direct evidence of policy-makers framing frontier allocation as an alternative to expropriation
  why it matters: this determines whether the claim is about intent or effect
  best note or source to pursue: congressional debates or ministry archives

- gap: direct measures of forest loss
  why it matters: this determines whether the environmental arm is evidenced or inferred
  best note or source to pursue: environmental-history notes or land-cover registries
