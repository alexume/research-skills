# Example: Variable–Mechanism Mismatch

## Problem

The manuscript defines extractivism as a process linked to:
- export specialization,
- insertion into international commodity markets,
- dependence on primary exports,
- and external accumulation dynamics.

However, the empirical analysis operationalises extractivism exclusively through Domestic Extraction (DE).

This creates a mismatch between:
- the conceptual definition,
and:
- the empirical variable.

Domestic Extraction measures the volume of extraction, but not necessarily:
- export orientation,
- dependency structures,
- enclave dynamics,
- or integration into unequal trade relations.

As a consequence, countries with large domestic extraction volumes but diversified economies could also appear “extractivist” under this operationalisation.

## Why this matters

The variable captures:
- extraction,
but not necessarily:
- extractivism as a political-economic structure.

This weakens:
- the coherence of the argument,
- the interpretation of the results,
- and the validity of the causal claims.

## Diagnostic Label

VARIABLE-MECHANISM MISMATCH

## Suggested Improvement

Clarify whether the paper studies:
- extraction,
or:
- extractivism.

If the latter, the analysis should incorporate variables related to:
- export dependence,
- commodity specialization,
- trade structure,
- or external orientation.

Alternatively, the conceptual framing should be narrowed to match the operationalised variable.
