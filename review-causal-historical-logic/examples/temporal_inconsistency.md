# Example: Temporal Inconsistency

## Problem

The manuscript analyses a long historical period as if institutions, development strategies, and economic structures remained stable throughout time.

The empirical analysis pools together observations from:
- import-substitution industrialisation,
- debt crisis adjustment,
- neoliberal restructuring,
- and export-led globalization,
without accounting for structural breaks or changing state capacities.

The paper assumes that the same causal relationship operated uniformly across all periods.

## Why this matters

Historical causality is temporally uneven.

Processes such as:
- trade integration,
- agrarian expansion,
- industrial policy,
- and environmental pressure
operate differently under distinct institutional and geopolitical regimes.

Ignoring periodisation risks:
- flattening historical dynamics,
- overstating causal continuity,
- and masking regime-specific mechanisms.

## Diagnostic Labels

TEMPORAL INCONSISTENCY

HISTORICAL FLATTENING

AHISTORICAL INFERENCE

## Suggested Improvement

The manuscript should:
- introduce clearer historical periodisation,
- test whether relationships vary across periods,
- discuss changing institutional contexts,
- and distinguish short-run from long-run dynamics.

At minimum, the discussion section should acknowledge that:
- causal mechanisms may differ across historical regimes,
- and that the observed relationship is unlikely to be historically uniform.
