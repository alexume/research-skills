# Journal Families

Use a journal family to calibrate argument development before a target journal is fixed. A family changes editorial questions, evidence priorities, and gate criteria; it is not a prose template or a substitute for checking the selected journal.

Set `manuscript.journal_family` in `project/project_profile.yaml`. Refine with `target_journal` when known.

## `development-environment`

Indicative journals: *World Development*, *Global Environmental Change*, *Development and Change*.

Prioritize:

- a development or socioenvironmental problem with relevance beyond the case;
- institutional and political-economic mechanisms;
- inequality, distribution, heterogeneity, and structural constraints;
- explicit connections across scales;
- governance or policy implications derived from results.

Check for generic Global South framing, underdeveloped mechanisms, policy added after the analysis, and a case whose wider contribution remains unclear.

## `ecological-economics-sustainability`

Indicative journals: *Ecological Economics*, *Journal of Cleaner Production*, and related social-metabolism, industrial-ecology, or sustainability journals.

Prioritize:

- rigorous definition and interpretation of biophysical indicators;
- stock-flow, fund-flow, scale, composition, displacement, and externalization;
- connections between biophysical processes, social relations, institutions, and political economy;
- differences between environmental accounting and ecological outcomes;
- implications for socioecological or production-system transitions.

When the target is closer to *Ecological Economics*, emphasize political economy, distribution, and conceptual contribution. When it is closer to *Journal of Cleaner Production*, emphasize production systems, operational implications, and transition pathways without weakening structural analysis.

Check for indicator overreach, technocratic sustainability claims, weak operationalization, and biophysical results detached from institutions and power.

## `economic-history`

Indicative journals: *Journal of Economic History*, *Economic History Review*, *European Review of Economic History*, *Explorations in Economic History*, and *Cliometrica*.

Prioritize:

- a precise historical question and historiographic intervention;
- periodization, comparison, and source construction;
- transparent measurement and inference;
- historically plausible mechanisms and actors;
- a contribution that extends beyond a new dataset or technique.

Check for technically credible but historically thin results, decorative context, anachronistic mechanisms, weak source criticism, and identification presented as the substantive contribution.

## `agrarian-environmental-political-economy`

Indicative journals: *Journal of Agrarian Change*, *Journal of Peasant Studies*, *Environmental History*, *Environment and History*, and *Geoforum*.

Prioritize:

- land, labor, class, state formation, conflict, and power;
- territorial and environmental transformation;
- commodity frontiers and production systems;
- concrete mediation between institutions, social relations, and biophysical processes;
- regional specificity, historical sequence, and coexistence of regimes.

Check for landscapes without actors, environment treated as an external effect, markets detached from agrarian relations, violence used only as context, and critical concepts without adequate historical evidence.

## Selection Rules

- Select the family that matches the paper's intended debate and evidentiary contribution, not the author's discipline alone.
- If two families are plausible, name one primary family and treat the other as a secondary audience in `submission/journal_fit.qmd`.
- Do not blend all family requirements into a maximal checklist.
- Use the exact target-journal profile to refine, not reverse, the family-level architecture.
- Record an unresolved family choice as an author decision before full discussion drafting.
- Keep family identifiers and calibration language out of manuscript prose. Translate the calibration into a substantive debate, audience, mechanism, or implication.
