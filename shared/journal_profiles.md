# Journal Profiles

Use `journal_families.md` for early argument and editorial calibration. Use the profiles below to refine the selected family when a target journal is known. Family modes and journal profiles guide editorial priorities; they do not prescribe reusable prose.

## Contents

- Economic history: *Economic History Review*, *Journal of Economic History*, *European Review of Economic History*, *Explorations in Economic History*, *Cliometrica*, and *Economic History of Developing Regions*.
- Development and environment: *World Development*, *Global Environmental Change*, and *Development and Change*.
- Ecological economics and production: *Ecological Economics* and *Journal of Cleaner Production*.
- Agrarian and environmental history: *Journal of Agrarian Change*, *Environmental History*, *Environment and History*, *Historia Agraria*, and *Agricultural History*.
- Latin American and comparative studies: *Latin American Research Review*, *Journal of Latin American Studies*, *Hispanic American Historical Review*, *Past & Present*, and *Comparative Studies in Society and History*.
- Political economy and geography: *Review of International Political Economy*, *Geoforum*, and *Journal of Peasant Studies*.

## Economic History Review
- Orientation: economic and social history with clear historical framing and strong empirical grounding.
- Expected contribution: a specific intervention in economic-history debates, often with comparative or longitudinal value.
- Methodological expectations: transparent sources, credible identification or careful historical inference, and clear contextualization.
- Common reviewer concerns: thin historiography, overclaiming from limited archives, weak causal logic, or insufficient historical periodization.

## Journal of Economic History
- Orientation: analytically rigorous economic history with strong engagement in economics and historical evidence.
- Expected contribution: a clear contribution to a substantive historical question with broader analytical relevance.
- Methodological expectations: well-specified empirical design, transparent data construction, and strong link between theory and evidence.
- Common reviewer concerns: identification that is clever but historically thin, poor mechanism discussion, limited robustness, or weak literature positioning.

## World Development
- Orientation: development problems with policy relevance, political economy, inequality, and global South perspectives.
- Expected contribution: a substantive empirical or conceptual contribution with implications for development debates.
- Methodological expectations: credible design, policy relevance, heterogeneity awareness, and careful interpretation of findings.
- Common reviewer concerns: generic framing, limited development relevance, underdeveloped mechanisms, or weak engagement with structural constraints.

## Journal of Agrarian Change
- Orientation: agrarian political economy, land, labor, rural transformation, and critical development scholarship.
- Expected contribution: a theoretically informed account of agrarian change grounded in concrete social relations.
- Methodological expectations: strong historical and institutional contextualization, attention to class, labor, and land relations, and careful case logic.
- Common reviewer concerns: depoliticized land dynamics, weak agrarian structure, vague concepts, or frontier narratives that ignore power and conflict.

## Global Environmental Change
- Orientation: major socio-environmental transformations with interdisciplinary relevance and strong explanatory framing.
- Expected contribution: a clear contribution to understanding environmental change, governance, adaptation, or sustainability transitions.
- Methodological expectations: strong conceptual framing, careful treatment of scale, and robust linkage between environmental indicators and social processes.
- Common reviewer concerns: scale mismatches, indicator overreach, weak mechanism, limited novelty, or underdeveloped policy relevance.

## Ecological Economics
- Orientation: biophysical political economy, sustainability, social metabolism, and critiques of growth-centered analysis.
- Expected contribution: a conceptually clear intervention in ecological-economic debates with strong empirical interpretation.
- Methodological expectations: rigorous indicator interpretation, attention to scale and externalization, and explicit links between environment and political economy.
- Common reviewer concerns: extraction-extractivism confusion, simplistic decoupling claims, weak operationalization, or insufficient historical context.

## Journal of Cleaner Production
- Orientation: cleaner production, sustainability transitions, industrial ecology, circularity, production systems, and operational environmental improvement.
- Expected contribution: an empirically grounded contribution that connects environmental performance or production-system change to actionable transition pathways.
- Methodological expectations: transparent indicators, reproducible analysis, clear system boundaries, and implications linked to the studied production or governance system.
- Common reviewer concerns: descriptive indicator exercises, generic sustainability recommendations, weak system boundaries, or technical solutions detached from institutional conditions.

## European Review of Economic History
- Orientation: comparative and analytical economic history with strong historical questions, transparent evidence, and relevance across cases or periods.
- Expected contribution: a historically specific empirical contribution that informs a broader economic-history debate.
- Methodological expectations: careful source and measurement work, credible inference, explicit comparison or periodization, and historically grounded mechanisms.
- Common reviewer concerns: narrow case relevance, weak historiographic positioning, limited source criticism, or technically sound results without historical payoff.

## Environmental History
- Orientation: historically grounded studies of human-environment relations, environmental change, state formation, science, technology, agriculture, extraction, and conservation.
- Expected contribution: a specific intervention in environmental historiography with clear periodization, source criticism, and environmental interpretation.
- Methodological expectations: careful use of archival, textual, quantitative, spatial, or material evidence; explicit historical context; and sensitivity to scale and causality.
- Common reviewer concerns: environmental processes treated as background, weak historiographic positioning, limited source criticism, or presentist sustainability framing.

## Environment and History
- Orientation: interdisciplinary environmental history with strong attention to socioecological processes, historical evidence, and conceptual framing.
- Expected contribution: a historically specific explanation that speaks to environmental-history, political-ecology, or socioecological transformation debates.
- Methodological expectations: strong source grounding, careful interpretation of environmental indicators, and explicit treatment of temporal and spatial scale.
- Common reviewer concerns: thin historical evidence, weak periodization, overly generic political ecology, or insufficient engagement with environmental-history literature.

## Historia Agraria
- Orientation: agrarian, rural, environmental, and food history, especially with strong empirical grounding and historical specificity.
- Expected contribution: a clear intervention in agrarian-history debates concerning land, labour, production, institutions, markets, conflict, or rural transformation.
- Methodological expectations: transparent sources, careful regional and temporal contextualization, and strong connection between agrarian structure and historical process.
- Common reviewer concerns: insufficient archival or statistical grounding, weak engagement with agrarian historiography, or overgeneralization from local evidence.

## Latin American Research Review
- Orientation: interdisciplinary Latin American studies, including history, political economy, development, environment, and social science.
- Expected contribution: a contribution relevant beyond a narrow case, with clear Latin Americanist positioning and substantive empirical grounding.
- Methodological expectations: accessible framing for interdisciplinary readers, transparent sources or data, and careful connection between case evidence and broader debates.
- Common reviewer concerns: too technical for interdisciplinary audiences, weak regional literature engagement, narrow case relevance, or unclear contribution.

## Journal of Latin American Studies
- Orientation: historically and socially grounded Latin American research with strong empirical contribution and regional relevance.
- Expected contribution: a paper that advances understanding of Latin American historical, social, economic, political, or environmental processes.
- Methodological expectations: strong contextualization, robust primary or empirical evidence, and clear engagement with Latin Americanist debates.
- Common reviewer concerns: weak country or regional historiography, overreliance on generic theory, or claims that exceed the case evidence.

## Hispanic American Historical Review
- Orientation: leading historical journal for Latin America, requiring strong historiographic contribution and high-quality source work.
- Expected contribution: a significant intervention in Latin American historiography, often with archival depth and conceptual precision.
- Methodological expectations: rigorous primary-source engagement, historical specificity, careful prose, and clear positioning in existing historiography.
- Common reviewer concerns: insufficient archival originality, overly social-scientific framing without historical depth, weak historiographic stakes, or thin periodization.

## Agricultural History
- Orientation: historical studies of agriculture, rural society, food systems, technology, land, and environmental change.
- Expected contribution: a historically grounded contribution to agricultural or rural history with clear empirical evidence.
- Methodological expectations: strong source base, attention to production systems and rural institutions, and clear historical interpretation.
- Common reviewer concerns: narrow descriptive case, insufficient rural-historical framing, weak connection between agricultural change and social or environmental processes.

## Past & Present
- Orientation: high-level historical scholarship with broad conceptual significance and strong comparative, temporal, or theoretical contribution.
- Expected contribution: an original historical argument with implications beyond a specialized subfield.
- Methodological expectations: exceptional source work, strong historiographic intervention, conceptual clarity, and persuasive narrative architecture.
- Common reviewer concerns: contribution too narrow, insufficient historiographic ambition, weak source originality, or social-science framing not translated into historical argument.

## Comparative Studies in Society and History
- Orientation: comparative, historical, anthropological, and social-scientific work with strong conceptual payoff.
- Expected contribution: a historically grounded argument that travels across cases, concepts, or comparative formations.
- Methodological expectations: explicit comparative logic, careful handling of scale and context, and strong theoretical framing rooted in evidence.
- Common reviewer concerns: comparison underdeveloped, case logic unclear, historical evidence too thin, or conceptual claims overextended.

## Explorations in Economic History
- Orientation: quantitative and analytical economic history with clear empirical strategy and contribution to economic-history debates.
- Expected contribution: a credible empirical contribution with transparent data, defensible identification or measurement, and historical interpretation.
- Methodological expectations: careful data construction, robustness, clear econometric design where relevant, and historically meaningful mechanisms.
- Common reviewer concerns: weak identification, insufficient historical motivation, limited external relevance, or underdeveloped mechanism.

## Cliometrica
- Orientation: cliometrics and quantitative economic history with emphasis on measurement, models, and empirical rigor.
- Expected contribution: a technically credible quantitative contribution to economic history, often with new data or formal empirical design.
- Methodological expectations: transparent replication materials, careful inference, robustness, and clear connection between empirical results and historical questions.
- Common reviewer concerns: underpowered analysis, weak data provenance, insufficient robustness, or historical contribution secondary to technique.

## Economic History of Developing Regions
- Orientation: economic history of Africa, Asia, Latin America, and other developing regions with empirical and historical grounding.
- Expected contribution: a substantive contribution to development-oriented economic history, often with regional specificity and broader implications.
- Methodological expectations: transparent data or source construction, contextualized interpretation, and clear connection to development-history debates.
- Common reviewer concerns: generic development framing, weak regional literature, insufficient source transparency, or overstated generalization.

## Review of International Political Economy
- Orientation: international political economy with attention to global capitalism, trade, finance, production networks, development, and power.
- Expected contribution: a theoretically explicit intervention in IPE debates with strong empirical grounding.
- Methodological expectations: clear conceptual contribution, strong political-economic mechanism, and attention to global structures and asymmetries.
- Common reviewer concerns: historical case too descriptive, insufficient IPE theory, weak causal or mechanism logic, or limited general relevance.

## Geoforum
- Orientation: critical human geography, political ecology, development, environment, territory, and spatial political economy.
- Expected contribution: a conceptually informed empirical argument about space, power, environment, economy, or territory.
- Methodological expectations: clear theoretical framing, strong case evidence, spatial sensitivity, and explicit engagement with geographical debates.
- Common reviewer concerns: weak spatial argument, underdeveloped theory, descriptive empiricism, or insufficient methodological transparency.

## Journal of Peasant Studies
- Orientation: critical agrarian studies, peasant politics, land, labour, food regimes, rural movements, and agrarian political economy.
- Expected contribution: a theoretically informed and politically grounded contribution to agrarian change debates.
- Methodological expectations: attention to class, land, labour, state, capital, conflict, and rural social relations; strong empirical or historical grounding.
- Common reviewer concerns: depoliticized agrarian change, weak class analysis, inadequate engagement with agrarian studies, or thin field/source evidence.

## Development and Change
- Orientation: critical development studies, political economy, inequality, state, environment, global capitalism, and social transformation.
- Expected contribution: a strong conceptual or empirical intervention in development debates with relevance beyond a single case.
- Methodological expectations: robust evidence, clear theoretical contribution, political-economic mechanism, and careful treatment of structural constraints.
- Common reviewer concerns: generic policy framing, weak theory, insufficient engagement with critical development literature, or overgeneralized claims.
