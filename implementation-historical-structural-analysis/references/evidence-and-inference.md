# Evidence and Historical-Structural Inference

## Evidence roles

- `pattern`: establishes what changed, where, and when.
- `sequence`: establishes temporal ordering and intermediate developments.
- `actor`: documents perception, strategy, capacity, conflict, or response.
- `institution`: documents rules, implementation, enforcement, or organizational change.
- `mechanism`: supports a link in the explanatory sequence.
- `contrastive`: weakens or differentiates a rival explanation.
- `scope`: defines where or when the explanation should apply.

One source can serve multiple roles only when each use is justified separately.

## Strength language

Use:

- `established` when convergent evidence directly supports the pattern or sequence within stated scope;
- `supported` when multiple evidence types fit the mechanism and important rivals are weakened;
- `plausible` when evidence is indirect, incomplete, or non-discriminating;
- `unresolved` when central contradictions or observation gaps remain.

Do not translate these labels mechanically into numeric scores.

## Mechanism assessment

For each link ask:

1. Could the evidence have observed the proposed process?
2. Is temporal ordering compatible?
3. Are actors and institutions observable rather than inferred from outcomes?
4. Does the link operate at the scale claimed?
5. Is there evidence inconsistent with the link?
6. Would the same evidence be expected under a rival explanation?

## Negative evidence

Absence supports an inference only when the source-production process, survival, and search strategy make observation likely. Otherwise classify it as unobserved, not absent.

## Uncertainty

Carry source, measurement, boundary, temporal, and interpretive uncertainty into the final wording. Quantitative precision cannot eliminate uncertainty in the construction or historical meaning of a measure.
