# Analytical Operations

## Temporal analysis

Use levels, shares, rates, indexes, rolling summaries, cumulative change, cohort profiles, or decomposition only when they match the concept and source frequency. Mark breaks in source definitions and coverage separately from historical turning points. Compare proposed periods using common measures and source regimes where possible.

## Spatial analysis

State the geographic unit, boundary version, aggregation rule, and spatial support. Examine concentration, clustering, diffusion, corridors, frontiers, and displacement without treating mapped proximity as a mechanism. Use boundary-harmonized and original-geography results when harmonization could alter the pattern.

## Compositional and distributional analysis

Separate aggregate growth from changes in shares, population composition, sector weights, land-use mix, commodity mix, or material intensity. Report whether observed transformation reflects entry, exit, reclassification, redistribution, or within-unit change.

## Institutional analysis

Translate statutes and formal reforms into implementation evidence: budgets, staffing, enforcement, jurisdiction, organizational practice, actor response, and temporal reach. Distinguish formal adoption from effective operation.

## Socioecological analysis

Keep stocks and flows, territory and trade, extraction and consumption, and gross and net measures distinct. Connect biophysical change to actors, property, labor, infrastructure, markets, state capacity, and distribution rather than presenting environmental indicators as autonomous drivers.

## Comparison log

For each comparison record:

- analytical purpose;
- cases or periods;
- common and differing dimensions;
- source and measurement comparability;
- expected discriminating evidence;
- observed result and limitations.
