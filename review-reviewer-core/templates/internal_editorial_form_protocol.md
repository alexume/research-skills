# Internal Editorial Form Protocol

Use this template when completing a fixed internal editorial evaluation form for a history journal.

## Purpose

The objective is not to produce a referee report for the author.
The objective is to help the handling editor decide whether the manuscript should be sent to external review.

The tone must be:

- concise
- professional
- analytically grounded
- editor-facing

Do not justify the decision by appealing to the prestige, ranking, or status of the journal.
Base the assessment on argument, evidence, method, structure, and maturity of the manuscript.

## Core Sequence

1. Reconstruct the article in 2-3 sentences.
2. State the main asset of the manuscript.
3. State the main reasons for caution or rejection.
4. Give a clear editorial recommendation.

## Fixed-Form Protocol

If the form contains closed yes/no questions that cannot be marked with an `X` because the checkbox area is an image:

- highlight in yellow only the questions the manuscript clearly satisfies
- leave unhighlighted the questions the manuscript does not clearly satisfy
- do not add separate inline answers unless the editor explicitly asks for them

If the form contains a proposal section that cannot be marked manually:

- highlight in yellow the chosen option label
- add one short line immediately below the proposal area stating the recommendation explicitly

## Author Comments Block

This block should begin with a brief description of the article.

Recommended structure:

1. What the article does
2. What is promising
3. What must be revised before external review

Preferred length:

- 2 to 3 short paragraphs

The comments should focus on:

- clarity of the research question
- quality and relevance of the sources
- comparability of cases if the article is comparative
- calibration of causal claims
- structure and argumentative economy

## Editor Comments Block

This block should function as a note to the handling editor.

Recommended structure:

1. One-sentence reconstruction of the manuscript
2. One-sentence recommendation
3. Two or three concrete reasons
4. One sentence on whether a deeply revised resubmission could be viable

Preferred length:

- 1 short paragraph or 3 to 5 sentences

## Style Rules

- Start from the manuscript, not from the verdict.
- Avoid generic praise.
- Avoid vague phrases such as `the level of the journal`, `prestige`, `top journal`, or similar.
- Prefer `the article does not yet sustain`, `the comparison remains asymmetric`, `the contribution is promising but not yet secured`.
- Distinguish clearly between:
  - an interesting topic
  - a valuable archive
  - a publishable argument

## Calibration Example

Good opening for author comments:

`The article compares two rural Castilian localities to assess how proximity to major urban centers shaped productive trajectories, cereal composition, and peasant inequality in the sixteenth and seventeenth centuries. Its main strength lies in the use of parish tithe records to reconstruct agrarian production at household scale.`

Good opening for editor comments:

`The manuscript addresses a potentially valuable question and rests on an attractive documentary base, but it does not yet align the strength of its causal claims with the evidence it actually presents.`

## Final Recommendation Labels

Use plain editorial language:

- send to external review
- do not send to external review
- invite deep resubmission before external review

Avoid overdesigned verdict language unless the form itself requires it.
