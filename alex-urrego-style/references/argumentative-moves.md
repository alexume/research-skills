# Argumentative Moves

Use this reference to calibrate reasoning across projects. It captures recurring authorial preferences, not a reusable project argument or a fixed paragraph template.

## Select Relations, Do Not Fill Slots

Begin from the current manuscript's evidence and question. Select only the moves that clarify its explanation.

### Empirical tension

Place two supported patterns in relation when their coexistence creates the problem the paper must explain. Do not manufacture opposition between independent trends.

### Historical mediation

Identify how actors, institutions, infrastructures, technologies, knowledge systems, and practices connect broad economic change with social and biophysical processes. Specify timing and territory.

### Temporal accumulation

Show how older arrangements persist, erode, combine with, or constrain newer ones. Prefer historically layered explanations to automatic substitution narratives when the evidence supports persistence.

### Scalar transformation

Explain how a relation changes when observed at household, local, regional, national, or global scale. Do not use `multiscalar` as a substitute for naming what changes between scales.

### Composition and aggregate

Use decomposition when different components carry different institutional, social, or ecological meanings. Explain why the aggregate changes interpretation once composition is visible.

### Process comparison

Compare cases through mechanisms, sequences, or institutional configurations rather than through parallel description alone. State both the common problem and the consequential difference.

### Socioecological articulation

Connect economic processes, social relations, and biophysical dynamics through concrete mediations. Do not treat nature as an external effect or institutions as an abstract list.

Test the connection explicitly: state which actor, institution, infrastructure, practice, or material dependency carries the relation, at what period and scale, and with what evidence. Adjacency across paragraphs is not articulation.

### Debate transformation

End the argumentative movement by stating what the combined evidence changes in an existing interpretation. Avoid generic claims of novelty or importance.

If the only remaining contribution is that an indicator or design has limits, test whether the combined evidence supports a stronger reformulation of the debate. When that reformulation requires a central missing source or author choice, request it before drafting.

## Evidence Discipline

For each selected move:

- name the evidence supporting each side of the relation;
- distinguish direct support from inferred synthesis;
- identify the scale and period of the bridge;
- consider a serious rival;
- request evidence when the bridge is central and retrievable;
- test whether narrowing or parking the bridge would collapse the promised contribution;
- state one material boundary after the positive interpretation.

## Rhythm

Allow a paragraph to carry several linked sentences when they develop one cumulative problem. Vary sentence and paragraph length with the analytical work. Avoid repeating the same `finding -> caveat` sequence across a section.

## Exclusions

- Do not reproduce argument chains from earlier projects.
- Do not imitate phrases from the authorial corpus.
- Do not force every paper to connect all disciplines in the author's research portfolio.
- Do not turn causal, historical-structural, or hybrid route labels into manuscript argument.
