# Scientific Prose Rules

These rules originated in a specific research project.
Apply the general principles across projects, but treat domain-specific replacements as examples rather than universal bans.

## Core Principle

Write what you mean. State the finding, then support it. Do not announce that you are about to state a finding.

## Cut These Always

### Throat-clearing openers

Remove phrases that delay the point. State the point directly.

- `The paper proceeds as follows...`
- `In this section, we...`
- `It is worth noting that...`
- `As we will see...`
- `I want to explore...`
- `Here's the thing:`
- `Let me be clear`

### Emphasis crutches

Delete empty emphasis.

- `Full stop.`
- `Period.`
- `Let that sink in.`
- `Make no mistake`
- `This matters because...`

### Vague declaratives

Replace generic statements with the actual implication, structure, stake, or consequence.

- `The implications are significant`
- `The reasons are structural`
- `The stakes are high`
- `The consequences are real`

### Stacked hedging

One qualifier can add precision. Several qualifiers usually weaken the claim.

- Bad: `This arguably suggests that the results may potentially indicate...`
- Better: `The results suggest...`
- Better: `The results show...`

### Performative contribution claims

Do not announce that a contribution is important. State the contribution.

- Bad: `This is a novel and significant contribution to the literature`
- Bad: `Together these findings respond directly to the gaps identified by...`

### Workflow leakage

Keep route choice, gate status, audit labels, and internal evidence classifications out of manuscript prose unless the reader needs them to evaluate method or inference. Translate the internal decision into the substantive claim it permits.

Keep journal-family identifiers and editorial calibration labels backstage. Name the substantive development, environmental, historical, distributional, or governance debate instead of writing that a result matters "for" a workflow family.

### Disclaimer chains

State the strongest warranted interpretation first. Add one material boundary where it changes the reading. Consolidate limitations rather than repeating variants of `does not identify`, `cannot establish`, or `is beyond the scope` across consecutive paragraphs.

If removing the disclaimers leaves no argument, return to argument development; do not disguise the gap with smoother prose.

### Contribution-preserving revision

Do not equate caution with subtraction. Before removing a central bridge, determine whether it is an unsupported overclaim, a plausible relation requiring evidence, or an authorial choice among defensible interpretations. If deletion leaves only an indicator audit or descriptive summary, return to argument development and evidence escalation.

## Register Rules

### Passive voice

- Allow passive voice in methods when conventional.
- Prefer active voice in results and discussion when a natural subject exists.

### Adverbs

Delete adverbs that intensify without adding meaning.

- Usually cut: `really`, `truly`, `genuinely`, `deeply`, `fundamentally`, `importantly`, `crucially`
- Keep only when accurate: `approximately`, `systematically`, `substantially`, `consistently`, `markedly`

### Register

- Keep impersonal academic register for submission prose.
- Allow more direct register in working notes, memos, or README files.

### Signposting

- Keep at most one functional transition sentence when needed.
- Avoid paragraph-by-paragraph signposting.

## Analytical Writing Rules

### One controlling analytical move per paragraph

State the main analytical move early when possible. Support it after.
Allow layered paragraphs when the sentences remain tightly connected by the same mechanism, empirical sequence, or interpretive problem.

### Cumulative discussion

A discussion paragraph should do more than restate a result. It should connect the result to another pattern, mechanism, historical sequence, scale, rival, or debate when the evidence supports that connection. Do not manufacture a relation merely to avoid descriptive prose.

When interdisciplinary integration is claimed, name what mediates the relation. Separate paragraphs on markets, institutions, territory, or biophysical indicators are not integrated merely because they occur in the same section.

### Binary contrasts

Use them only when the contrast is itself analytically meaningful.

- Bad: `This is not a limitation. It is a structural feature.`
- Better: `The sensitivity is a structural feature of the oil palm coefficient range, not a data quality problem.`

Maximum: two binary contrasts per section.

### Specifics over abstractions

Every conceptual sentence should contain at least one specific referent.

- Bad: `The agrarian transition reconfigured territorial water pressure`
- Better: name crops, places, periods, and measured changes directly.

### Evaluable claims

Every major claim or sentence cluster should be supported by:

- a number,
- a citation,
- or a mechanism.

## Project-Specific Replacements

| Avoid | Replace with |
|---|---|
| `ecological pressure` alone | Specify `m3/ha`, depletion rate, or pressure type |
| `agrarian transformation` alone | Specify crop substitution, area expansion, or other named change |
| `heterogeneity` alone | Specify which countries, direction, and period |
| `multiscalar` | Name the scales and what differs across them |
| `nexus` | Name the connection |
| `sustainability implications` | State the implication |
| `policy-relevant` | State the policy |
| `this paper contributes to` | State the contribution directly |
| `the literature has shown` | Cite the specific paper and finding |
| `navigate` | `handle` or `address` |
| `unpack` | `explain` or `examine` |
| `deep dive` | `analysis` or `examination` |
| `landscape` in a non-literal sense | `situation`, `field`, or `context` |

## Quick Scoring

Score a draft from 1 to 10 on each dimension:

- Specificity: does every claim have a number, mechanism, or citation?
- Directness: are claims stated early and supported after?
- Rhythm: do sentence lengths vary without becoming choppy?
- Register: does the prose match journal conventions?
- Density: can anything still be cut without losing content?

Revise before sharing if the total is below 35 out of 50.
