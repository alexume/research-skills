# Authorial Revision

Use this protocol after an AI-assisted draft, a substantial restructuring, or any revision that may have replaced the author's reasoning with generic academic prose.

The objective is intellectual ownership and fidelity to the manuscript.
Do not optimize the text for AI detectors.

## Required Inputs

Use the strongest available combination of:

- the author's notes or outline,
- claim-evidence or mechanism maps,
- tables, figures, archival extracts, or analysis outputs,
- an earlier author-written draft,
- relevant authorial reference texts,
- and explicit decisions made during the project.

If none of these exists, flag that authorial calibration is weak.

## Checks

### Intellectual Ownership

- Can the author defend every interpretation and transition?
- Does each major claim derive from visible evidence or an explicit interpretive decision?
- Has fluent connective prose introduced a claim that was never decided?
- Are uncertainty and disagreement preserved where the research remains unresolved?

### Manuscript Specificity

- Could this paragraph appear unchanged in another paper?
- Does it name the relevant place, period, actors, evidence, mechanism, or debate?
- Are generic contribution statements replacing the actual contribution?
- Are broad concepts doing work that should be performed by historical detail?

### Authorial Rhythm

- Does sentence and paragraph length vary with analytical function?
- Are consecutive paragraphs built with the same template?
- Are numbered transitions used because the argument requires them or because the draft was generated schematically?
- Has editing removed useful historical texture or a distinctive analytical sequence?

### Claim Calibration

- Does the abstract, introduction, or conclusion promise more than the evidence supports?
- Are descriptive patterns distinguished from causal, structural, and interpretive claims?
- Are citations carrying the exact burden assigned to them?

### Intervention Record

For substantial AI-assisted writing, keep a short record of:

- source materials used,
- sections drafted or restructured with assistance,
- major interpretive decisions made by the author,
- claims qualified or removed,
- and unresolved points left for author review.

This can be recorded in `paper/authorial_revision_log.md` when the project needs formal traceability.

## Output

Return:

1. revised prose,
2. a short list of substantive author decisions still required,
3. any claims or transitions that lack traceable support,
4. and, when requested, an authorial revision log.
