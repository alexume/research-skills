# Skill: Mechanism Mapping

You connect observed patterns to plausible historical mechanisms.

This skill is used after EDA, source assessment, structural analysis design, or causal design. It is especially important for historical-structural and hybrid papers.

## Core principle

Patterns do not explain themselves. A publishable explanation requires mechanisms that specify how and through whom change occurred, followed by a synthesis of how those mechanisms interact.

## Workflow

### Step 1: List patterns
For each pattern, state:
- what changed,
- where,
- when,
- at what scale,
- how strong the pattern is.

### Step 2: Identify mechanisms
For each pattern, propose one or more mechanisms.

A mechanism should specify:
- actors,
- institutions,
- infrastructures, technologies, knowledge systems, or practices when relevant,
- resources,
- incentives,
- constraints,
- mediating processes,
- timing.

Ask which institutions and practices connect economic processes, social relations, and biophysical processes. Do not presume that all three domains are relevant in every case.

### Step 3: Link evidence
For each mechanism, identify supporting evidence and its limits.

### Step 4: Define rival explanations
For each mechanism, specify at least one alternative explanation.

### Step 5: Assess strength
Classify evidence as:
- high,
- medium,
- tentative,
- insufficient.

### Step 6: Build relational synthesis

After mapping mechanisms individually, assess relations among the major patterns and processes. Consider, without forcing:

- tension or contradiction,
- co-constitution,
- direct or institutional mediation,
- displacement across groups or territories,
- temporal accumulation or path dependence,
- feedback,
- process comparison,
- scalar transformation.

Identify the few relations with the highest explanatory yield. State how the combined interpretation changes the answer to the research question or the relevant debate.

When the paper claims integration across economic, social, institutional, or biophysical domains, name the mediation that connects them and the evidence for the sequence. Adjacency is not integration. Do not require domains that are irrelevant to the question.

Distinguish lack of econometric identification from lack of historical explanation. A structural or interpretive mechanism may be defensible when actors, sequence, scope, sources, rivals, and uncertainty are explicit.

### Step 7: Recommend next action
For weak mechanisms, specify whether to:
- acquire new evidence,
- request an author decision when competing mechanisms imply different contributions,
- qualify the claim,
- reframe as descriptive,
- park the mechanism,
- drop the claim.

## Diagnostic labels

Use selectively:
- WEAK MECHANISM
- OMITTED MEDIATION
- TEMPORAL INCONSISTENCY
- SCALAR INCOHERENCE
- INSTITUTIONAL HOMOGENISATION
- VARIABLE-MECHANISM MISMATCH
- DESCRIPTIVE-CAUSAL CONFUSION
- HISTORICAL FLATTENING
- PARALLEL MECHANISMS
- MISSING SYNTHESIS
- FORCED DIALECTIC

## Rules

- Do not allow a trend to substitute for a mechanism.
- Do not allow a mechanism without actors or institutions unless the paper explicitly justifies a structural abstraction.
- Always distinguish direct mechanisms from mediated mechanisms.
- Always state timing and scale.
- Always include at least one competing explanation for major claims.
- Do not stop at one-pattern-to-one-mechanism mapping when the research question requires an integrated explanation.
- Do not force tension, dialectic, or feedback where the evidence shows independent processes.
- Do not park a weak central mechanism solely to obtain a safer argument when a specific retrievable source or author decision could preserve the contribution.
- Keep project-specific causal chains in project artifacts, not in reusable skill instructions.
